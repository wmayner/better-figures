**Three attention heads, L9H6, L9H9 and L10H0, account for nearly all of the ablation effect.** Each of the model's 144 heads (12 layers × 12 heads) was ablated one at a time. We measured the resulting increase in loss, averaged over 500 prompts. **(a)** Loss increase by layer and head, on a linear colour scale that starts at 0. The three labelled cells are the only heads whose ablation raises the loss by more than 0.07: L9H6 (1.25), L9H9 (0.91) and L10H0 (0.72). All three sit in layers 9–10. A few heads have slightly negative estimates (the lowest is −0.022, and none differs significantly from zero); these are drawn in the zero colour. **(b)** The same heads ranked by loss increase. Error bars show 95% confidence intervals (±1.96 × SEM across prompts). The effect drops sharply after the top three. Each of the other 141 heads changes the loss by at most 0.064 (median 0.017), which is 11–20× smaller than the top three. Many of these small effects are reliably above zero, but the three named heads are the only ones with large effects.
