**Three attention heads in layers 9–10 account for most of the effect of single-head ablation on loss.** Each of the model's 144 heads (12 layers × 12 heads) was ablated one at a time. The increase in loss was averaged over 500 prompts. **(a)** Mean loss increase for each head, plotted by layer and head on a linear color scale starting at 0. The three cells with values printed in them are the only heads that stand out: L9H6 (1.25), L9H9 (0.91) and L10H0 (0.72). The 20 heads with slightly negative values (as low as −0.022; none more than 1.5 SEM below zero) are drawn in the lightest color. **(b)** All heads ranked by mean loss increase. Error bars are 95% confidence intervals (±1.96 SEM across prompts). The third-ranked head's increase is about 11 times that of the fourth (0.064), and together the top three make up 54% of the summed loss increase across all heads. The remaining 141 heads have a median increase of 0.017. Some of them are statistically above zero (up to 5.9 SEM), but their effects are more than an order of magnitude smaller than those of the top three.
