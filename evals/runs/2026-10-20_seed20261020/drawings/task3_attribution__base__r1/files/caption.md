**Integrated gradients agrees best with ablation on all three models.** Each row is an attribution method. Each marker is the Spearman rank correlation (ρ) between that method's per-head attribution scores and the measured effect of ablating each head, for GPT-2 small (circles, 144 heads), Pythia-410M (squares, 384 heads) and Gemma-2-2B (diamonds, 208 heads). Horizontal bars are confidence intervals. Rows are sorted by ρ averaged over the three models, shown on the right. The methods rank in the same order on every model: integrated gradients (ρ = 0.80–0.89), attribution patching (0.60–0.64), gradient × input (0.42–0.48), saliency (0.26–0.33) and activation magnitude (0.15–0.19). On every model, the integrated-gradients interval lies entirely above the attribution-patching interval. Bayes factors (BF₁₀) for a nonzero correlation are decisive for the top three methods (all > 10⁶). For saliency they range from 13.8 (GPT-2 small) to 3.3 × 10⁴. For activation magnitude they are only 0.8–3.6, so the evidence that it tracks ablation at all is inconclusive.
