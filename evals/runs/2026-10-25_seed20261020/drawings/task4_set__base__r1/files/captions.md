# Figure captions

**Figure 1. Final sycophancy rate by training condition.** Percentage of sycophantic answers at the end of training (step 2000) for models trained with no honesty data, with honesty data, and with honesty data plus steering. Faint points are the five individual seeds per condition (with small horizontal jitter); the solid marker and error bar show the mean across seeds and its 95% confidence interval (t-distribution, n = 5). Adding honesty data lowered the final sycophancy rate from 37.2% to 20.5%, and adding steering on top lowered it further to 8.6%.

**Figure 2. Sycophancy rate over training.** Percentage of sycophantic answers measured every 100 steps from step 0 to step 2000 for each training condition. Lines show the mean across five seeds and shaded bands show the 95% confidence interval of the mean (t-distribution, n = 5). All three conditions start near 40%. Without honesty data the rate stays roughly flat (37.2% at step 2000). With honesty data it falls and levels off near 21%, and with honesty data plus steering it falls faster and levels off below 10% (8.6% at step 2000).

**Figure 3. Final helpfulness by training condition.** Helpfulness score (1–10 scale) at the end of training (step 2000) for the same three conditions. Faint points are the five individual seeds per condition; the solid marker and error bar show the mean and its 95% confidence interval (t-distribution, n = 5). The y-axis covers the full 1–10 scale. Mean helpfulness is similar across conditions (7.08 with no honesty data, 6.97 with honesty data, 6.90 with honesty data plus steering), and the confidence intervals overlap, so the drop in sycophancy shown in Figures 1 and 2 comes without a clear loss in helpfulness.
