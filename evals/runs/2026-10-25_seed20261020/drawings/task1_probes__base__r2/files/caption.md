**Figure 1. Concepts become linearly decodable at different depths of the model.** Held-out linear-probe accuracy at each of the model's 24 layers (0–23) for four concepts (n = 2,000 test examples per probe). Lines show accuracy; shaded bands show the confidence interval for each estimate. The dashed line marks chance (50%). Each dot marks the layer where the concept *becomes decodable*: the first layer from which the lower bound of the confidence interval stays above chance at every later layer. The concepts emerge one after another: part of speech is decodable almost immediately (layer 1) and reaches its plateau of about 93–96% by layer 7. Sentiment follows (layer 5; plateau by layer 11), then named-entity type (layer 10; plateau by layer 17). Statement truthfulness appears last (layer 17) and is still rising at the final layer (91%), so it may not have reached its plateau within the model's depth. Once each concept rises, it climbs from near chance to near its plateau in about 5–6 layers.
