# Figure captions

**Figure 1. Final sycophancy rate by training condition.** Percentage of sycophantic answers at the end of training (step 2000) for models trained without honesty data, with honesty data, and with honesty data plus steering. Light dots are the five individual seeds. The darker point and vertical line are the mean across seeds and its 95% confidence interval (t-distribution, n = 5). Adding honesty data lowered the final sycophancy rate from 37.2% (95% CI ±3.5) to 20.5% (±1.1). Adding steering on top lowered it further, to 8.6% (±1.4). The seed ranges of the three conditions do not overlap.

**Figure 2. Sycophancy rate over training.** Percentage of sycophantic answers every 100 training steps, from step 0 to step 2000. Lines are the mean across five seeds, and shaded bands are 95% confidence intervals (t-distribution, n = 5). All three conditions start near 40%. Without honesty data the rate stays roughly flat and ends at 37.2%. With honesty data it falls steadily and levels off near 21% by about step 1500. With honesty data and steering it falls faster and further, levelling off near 9% from about step 1300.

**Figure 3. Final helpfulness by training condition.** Helpfulness score (1–10 scale) at the end of training (step 2000). Light dots are the five individual seeds. The darker point and vertical line are the mean across seeds and its 95% confidence interval (t-distribution, n = 5). Note that the y-axis shows only the 6–8 portion of the scale, so the seed-to-seed spread is visible. Mean helpfulness was 7.08 (±0.26) without honesty data, 6.97 (±0.11) with honesty data, and 6.90 (±0.24) with honesty data and steering. The confidence intervals overlap, and neither intervention differed reliably from the no-honesty-data baseline (Welch's t-test, p = 0.32 and p = 0.19). With five seeds per condition, a small drop in helpfulness cannot be ruled out.
