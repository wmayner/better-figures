**Three attention heads in layers 9–10 account for most of the model's sensitivity to head ablation.** Each of the 144 attention heads (12 layers × 12 heads) was ablated individually, and the resulting increase in loss was averaged over 500 prompts. **(a)** Mean increase in loss for each head, on a linear color scale. Ablating L9H6, L9H9 or L10H0 increases loss by 1.25, 0.91 and 0.72 respectively. Every other head's effect is at most 0.064 (median 0.017), so those cells look nearly blank at this scale. Dots mark the 9 other heads whose effect is significantly different from zero (two-sided z-test on mean/SEM, Bonferroni-corrected across 144 heads, p < 0.05). These effects are real but small (0.027–0.063), about 20× smaller than the weakest of the top three. Twenty heads have small negative means (minimum −0.022), none significant; the color scale clips them to 0. **(b)** The 15 heads with the largest effects, ranked, with 95% confidence intervals (mean ± 1.96 SEM). The three dominant heads (blue) are clearly separated from the rest, and every other head's effect is near zero.
